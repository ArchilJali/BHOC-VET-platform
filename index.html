Hemoglobin & other oxygen-carrying pigments

Nature uses more than one oxygen strategy.

Hemoglobin

Iron-containing heme protein and the central oxygen carrier across almost all vertebrate life.

Hemocyanin

Copper-based oxygen carrier found in many molluscs and arthropods.

Hemerythrin

Non-heme iron oxygen-binding protein found in several marine invertebrate groups.

Chlorocruorin

Greenish extracellular respiratory pigment found in certain marine polychaete worms.

Erythrocruorin

Large extracellular hemoglobin complex found in annelids and some other invertebrates.

Antarctic icefish

A notable vertebrate exception: adult crocodile icefish lack functional hemoglobin and rely on specialized cold-water physiology.

Heme and chlorophyll: both belong to related tetrapyrrole-ring chemistry, but they are not structurally identical. Heme coordinates iron (Fe); chlorophyll coordinates magnesium (Mg). Similar chemical ancestry is not biological equivalence.
Evidence bridge

From established search history to a developing category.

Veterinary need→Oxyglobin→HBOC→BHOC→Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier→Precision Oxygen Therapeutics
Scientific and regulatory boundary: Oxyglobin approval is for dogs. It does not establish approval for cats, horses, wildlife, avian species, camelids or BHOC. Off-label and translational evidence must be labelled. Shared oxygen biology does not make one formulation suitable for every species. Physiology, dosing, safety and regulatory evidence remain essential. Donor blood remains multifunctional; an oxygen carrier supports oxygen delivery and does not replace all blood functions. “Blood substitute” is retained only as historical search terminology and requires this clarification.
